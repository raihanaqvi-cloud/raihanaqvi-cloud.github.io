THE CASE ↓
THE PROBLEM

EDITING KEPT TURNING INTO TICKETS.

Editing a live product was a frustrating journey. Users couldn’t tell how the system was meant to work, so routine edits kept ending up as support tickets. Three pain points sat underneath it:

01
DUPLICATE LINE ITEMS

Every edit to a published product created a separate draft, marked only with a Draft pill. The same product showed up as several unrelated rows.

02
EDITS BY ACCIDENT

Clicking Edit in a row’s menu created an editable draft. Changing a published product and clicking Save turned it into a draft too.

03
NO REAL VERSION HISTORY

The only way back was rolling back one version at a time, and each rollback made another copy as a new version that someone then had to publish.

so what was really going on?
THE FINDING

NOBODY HAD DESIGNED THE EDITING JOURNEY.

The three pain points were three steps of the same journey: starting an edit, seeing what changed, and going back. Each step had been built on its own, and none of them said what was happening.

THE FIX

SHOW THE STATE. PREVENT THE SLIP.my pitch

Clear state, honest feedback and a guard against mistakes at every step of the edit.

01
UNPUBLISHED CHANGES
STATE VISIBILITY

A second pill marks a pending edit on the live product.

02
DISCARD DRAFTS
USER CONTROL

Throw away a pending change, instead of keeping another row.

03
VERSION HISTORY
FEEDBACK & RECOVERY

Every version, dated. Recent ones can be retrieved.

04
READ-ONLY VIEWS
ERROR PREVENTION

Published products open read-only, under a blue banner.

THE CONSTRAINT

only two steps back

Our system could only retrieve the last two versions. Rather than hide that, the interface never promises a way back that the system can’t deliver.

VALIDATE · STAKEHOLDER REVIEW

PITCHED, REVIEWED, APPROVED.

I pitched it to the CTO, the CMO and the product owners in an internal stakeholder review. The modal was approved and went to production. 

feedback after 2 months of use
CHANGE REQUEST

SHOW THE CHANGES, NOT THE LIVE DATA.

After two months of use, the client asked for one change. For a product with unpublished changes, the product table was showing the published product’s data. They wanted it to show the unpublished changes data instead.

BEFORE · TABLE SHOWED
PRODUCT AUnpublished changesPUBLISHED DATA
→
AFTER · TABLE SHOWS
PRODUCT AUnpublished changesUNPUBLISHED CHANGES DATA
THE OUTCOME

SUPPORT STOPPED ESCALATING IT.

After launch, support stopped escalating draft confusion to product. Edits to live products now happen on purpose.

HOW THE PILL SHIPPED

UI first, model later. The pill shipped in the interface first, then moved into the data model.

WHAT I’D DO DIFFERENTLY
lesson kept

Watch them use it, don’t just show it. The client’s end users did see the designs before launch, but looking at screens only drew vague feedback. Seeing people actually use a product brings out real data, the kind no review can. Since then we’ve brought in a business analyst and changed our design process.
